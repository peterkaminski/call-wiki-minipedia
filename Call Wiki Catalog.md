## Richness Comparison: Old-Style vs. Call Wiki

The contrast between a traditional AI meeting summary and a call wiki is stark:

A **traditional AI summary** of a one-hour call produces 1-3 pages: an overview, a bullet-point list of topics, and perhaps action items. It captures the *what* but not the *how* or *why*. It's flat — no connections between ideas, no way to explore a tangent that interested you.

A **call wiki** of the same one-hour call produces 50-150+ interconnected pages. Each concept, each person, each referenced work gets its own page with context, connections, and cross-references. You can enter the wiki from any angle — a person's name, a concept, a thread from the chat — and navigate to related material. The wiki captures not just what was said but the *texture* of the conversation: who introduced an idea, who built on it, where the group agreed or disagreed, what was mentioned in passing versus explored at length.

The difference is the difference between a table of contents and a library.
